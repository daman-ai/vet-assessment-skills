# Validation standards, ratings and scales

The compliance substance the documents encode. Use it to judge whether a change is
defensible, not just tidy.

## Two obligations, not one

They are routinely run together and called "validation". They are different, and the
evidence for each is different.

| | Standard 1.3(b) — review prior to use | Standard 1.5 — validation of judgements |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Could this tool, as written, gather evidence that satisfies the rules? | Do the judgements actually made hold up on the evidence recorded? |
| Needs | The unit, the tool, the study guide | Completed, marked student assessments |
| Students required | No | Yes |
| Recorded at | Form Part B | Form Part C |
| Produced by | Pre-Assessment Master Prompt (4304) | Post-Assessment Master Prompt (4307) |

A tool cannot be issued until it has passed the 1.3(b) review. A training product cannot be
signed off as validated until 1.5 has run on real judgements.

**Part A is neither.** It records the panel and its independence — facts about people. Never
generate its content; output a blank skeleton.

## Independence — Standard 1.5(e) and (f)

- The outcome of a validation **must not be determined solely** by a person who designed or
  delivered the training or assessment.
- The Lead Validator must not have designed, delivered or assessed the product being validated.
- People who designed or delivered **may take part** — their knowledge is useful — but cannot
  determine the outcome alone.
- At least one panel member must hold a Credential Policy validation credential (TAE40122 /
  TAE40116 / TAE40110, an Assessor Skill Set, a secondary teaching qualification plus
  TAESS00024, or a diploma or higher in adult education/VET).
- The panel collectively needs industry competence for the product and a practical
  understanding of how the work is done now.
- **For any TAE product enabling assessment judgements, validation after the first cohort
  must be by a person wholly external to the organisation.** A cross-brand reciprocal
  arrangement does not reach this.
- An unsigned Part A means the validation is not complete.

## Risk model

Score each factor 0–3, multiply by weight, total. **24+ High, 12–23 Medium, below 12 Low.**

| Factor | Weight | Scores high when |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment tool status | 3 | Tools are non-compliant, or have never passed a 1.3(b) review |
| Delivery status | 3 | A cohort is being assessed now on unreviewed tools |
| Licensing or safety outcome | 2 | Licensing outcome, or food safety / WHS content |
| Cohort vulnerability | 2 | International or EAL cohorts, or learners new to VET |
| Training product currency | 1 | Changed release, or approaching transition |
| Feedback and complaints | 1 | Assessor, student or industry feedback has flagged assessment |

A product **not currently delivered** scores 0 on both delivery status and cohort
vulnerability — that is a 12-point swing and usually moves it out of High. Rescore rather
than assuming an existing rating still holds.

## Sample sizes

Set by the Plan (4306). Prompts apply them; they do not invent them.

**Units per training product** — the regulatory floor is two units when validating a
qualification; everything above that is RTO policy.

| Rating | Units | Selection |
|---|---|---|
| High | 4, or 20%, whichever is greater | Weighted to licensing/safety outcomes, high failure or completion rates, practical observation |
| Medium | 3, or 15%, whichever is greater | A spread — at least one core, one elective, one practical |
| Low | 2 | One core, one elective, rotated across cycles |

**Completed assessments per unit**

| Rating | Sample | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| High | 15, or all if fewer | At least one per assessor and per site, and a mix of C and NYC outcomes |
| Medium | 10, or all if fewer | At least one per assessor |
| Low | 6, or all if fewer | Random from completed judgements |

A sample of only Competent outcomes cannot detect the failure that matters most — competence
recorded where it was not demonstrated. Say so if that is what was supplied.

## Judgement and severity scales

**Part B mapping judgements**

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| MET | Fully addressed and genuinely assessable as written |
| PARTIAL | Addressed but incomplete, ambiguous, or not assessable as written |
| NOT MET | Absent from the tool |

The depth test: match the cognitive verb. "Identify" may be satisfied by recall; "explain",
"describe", "analyse", "evaluate" may not. A recall question against an "explain" requirement
is PARTIAL. This is the single most common defect in VET assessment tools.

**Part C re-marking**

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SUPPORTED | The evidence on the record carries the outcome |
| NOT SUPPORTED | The evidence does not carry the outcome |
| CANNOT BE DETERMINED | The record is incomplete in a way that makes the question unanswerable — treat as Not supported for severity |

**Severity — the two scales differ, and the wording must not be carried across**

| | Part B | Part C |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | The tool cannot be used until this is fixed | The judgement is not supported. A learner holds an outcome that cannot be defended; reassessment or re-issue required |
| Major | Must be fixed before the next cohort | The judgement stands, but the record does not evidence it properly |
| Minor | Improvement only, no compliance risk | Record keeping or practice improvement, no risk to the judgement |

Any Critical finding at Part B forces the outcome "Not approved — rework and re-validate
before issue".

## Tool defect vs assessor defect

The judgement that makes a Part C report useful rather than accusatory:

- The tool gave no way to record what the unit requires → **tool defect**. Raise as an
  assessment-system finding against the pack.
- The tool provided for it and the assessor did not use it → **practice defect**. Raise
  against the judgement.
- Both → say both, and say which must be fixed first.

An assessor cannot be at fault for failing to record on a form with no field for it.

## Closure

Steps 9–14 of the Fifteen Steps. An entry is not closed until **implementation is evidenced
and effectiveness is verified**. "Confirm the amendment was made" tests completion, not
effect — a valid effectiveness test for a Part B entry is usually a Part C validation of the
first cohort.

Standard 1.5(g) requires evidence that validation outcomes actually changed the assessment
system. A finding recorded and not acted on is worse than no finding: it shows the problem
was known.

## Systemic findings

A defect recurring across three or more units is a property of the template or the practice,
not of the unit it was noticed in. Raise **one** assessment-system entry rather than three
tool findings, and fix it at template level — that closes many unit findings at once and is
where most of the leverage sits.
