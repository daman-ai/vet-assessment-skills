# Writing the feedback

Feedback appears in three places, at three lengths. All three come from the
ledger, so they cannot contradict each other.

| Where | Length | Written to |
|---|---|---|
| SAR, per tool row | 2–4 sentences | the student |
| Marking record, Comments column | a short phrase | the assessor and auditor |
| Student Feedback Sheet, per item | 1–2 sentences per cell | the student |

## The two-comma rule

**No sentence of assessor-written feedback carries more than two commas.** It
applies to SAR feedback, to both cells of every feedback sheet item, to the
marking record comment, and to resit feedback. The resolver blocks the build and
names the sentence.

A student reading a resubmission notice is being asked to act. A sentence with
four commas has to be read twice before it can be acted on, and many of these
students are reading in a second language. Two short sentences always beat one
long one:

> ✗ Your mousse card records no setting time, no holding temperature, and the
>   caramel was not checked for colour, so two requirements are not met.
>
> ✓ Your mousse card records no setting time and no holding temperature. The
>   caramel was not checked for colour. Two requirements are not yet met.

**This is not the authorship rule.** That one counts more than *four* commas in
a *student's* response and flags possible AI generation. This one counts more
than *two* in *our own* prose and blocks the build. Different target, different
limit, different consequence — see
[marking-standard.md](marking-standard.md#suspected-ai-generated-responses).

## In the SAR — two to four sentences per tool

Each one:

1. **Names what the student actually did** — reference the specific task,
   recipe, question or observation. Not "your work"; "your chocolate mousse
   recipe card".
2. **States plainly whether it met the requirements of the unit.**
3. **Where NYS, states exactly what must be corrected**, in terms the student
   can act on.
4. **Where NYS, states the resubmission date.**

Write to the student, in plain professional English, second person — "You
demonstrated…", "You need to…". No jargon, no assessor shorthand, no reference
to other students.

**Do not comment on the student's spelling, grammar or written expression.**
See [marking-standard.md](marking-standard.md). A feedback line that says "watch
your grammar" contradicts the standard the work was judged against and the
standing text on the student's own feedback sheet.

### Where nothing was submitted

Factual and brief: that no assessment was submitted for that tool, that the
outcome is NYS on that basis, and the resubmission date. Nothing else — there is
no work to comment on, and inventing commentary about absent work is inventing
evidence.

> No assessment was submitted for the Knowledge Questions, so this assessment is
> Not Yet Satisfactory on that basis. Submit your completed Knowledge Questions
> by 09 / 09 / 2026.

### A worked Satisfactory

> You produced the crème caramel, the chocolate mousse and the poached pear to
> the standard recipes. You were observed portioning and plating all three within
> the service period. Your mise en place and your wastage control met the
> requirements of the unit. This assessment is satisfactory.

### A worked Not Yet Satisfactory

> You produced all three desserts and your poached pear met the standard recipe.
> Your chocolate mousse card records no setting time and no holding temperature.
> The crème caramel was served without a colour check on the caramel. Two of the
> unit's requirements are not yet demonstrated. Correct the two items on your
> feedback sheet and resubmit both attempts by 09 / 09 / 2026.

Note what it does: names the dishes, says what met the standard *before* what
did not, names the two missing requirements specifically, and points to the
sheet rather than repeating it. No sentence carries more than two commas.

## In the marking record — a short phrase

Enough to identify the issue at a glance in a column read down, not across.

- `All tasks satisfactory`
- `Recipe workbook: 2 dishes not to standard`
- `Q6 flagged — not own work`
- `No submission` — **exactly this string**, where nothing was submitted

The resolver rejects a comment over 120 characters.

## On the Student Feedback Sheet — one or two sentences per cell

The sheet is the document the student receives. It must be readable on its own
without the SAR. One row per question or task to redo.

**Issue identified** — be specific about the *requirement not met*. Not
"incorrect", but what was needed:

> The recipe card records no setting time and no holding temperature. The unit
> requires you to record both for a set dessert.

**What you need to do** — plain, actionable, and finishable:

> Add the setting time in hours and the holding temperature in °C to recipe card
> 2, and resubmit the card.

Where a response was flagged for authorship, the issue reads exactly:

> Response does not appear to be your own work — rewrite in your own words.

### More than ten items

The sheet holds ten items on one page. Where a student has more, list the ten
most significant and let the builder add the closing note that the remaining
items are marked in the returned assessment. Do not shrink the type or spill to
a second page.
