## The two obligations

The distinction the session turns on. Trainers and assessors routinely collapse these, and separating them is the single most useful thing the session does.

| | Standard 1.3(b) — review prior to use | Standard 1.5 — validation |
|---|---|---|
| What is examined | The tool itself, before any learner sees it | Assessment practices and the judgements assessors made |
| What you need in hand | The unit, the tool, the learner materials | Completed, marked learner assessments |
| When it can happen | Any time, but before first use | Only after a cohort has been assessed |
| Who can do it | Assessors, including the tool's author, though a second reader is better practice | Not determined solely by anyone who designed, delivered or assessed the product |
| How often | Every tool, every time it is new or changed | Each training product at least once every five years, sooner on risk, product change or feedback |

Standard 1.3(b) requires that assessment tools are reviewed prior to use against the principles of assessment and the rules of evidence, and that the outcome of that review drives changes to the tool. A review that finds nothing and changes nothing is not evidence of compliance.

Standard 1.4 supplies the yardstick — the principles and the rules are named as the test.

Under Standard 1.5, the risk-based approach determines which components are validated and the sample size. The practice guide sets one hard floor: at least two units of competency when validating a qualification, short course or skill set.

## Principles of assessment

Teach these as failure modes. Everyone has heard the definitions.

| Principle | What it requires | What a breach looks like in a tool |
|---|---|---|
| Validity | Assesses what the unit says, at the level the unit sets | A performance criterion appears in no instrument. A question tests recall where the unit requires application |
| Reliability | Different assessors reach the same judgement on the same evidence | No benchmark answer. An observation row reading "demonstrates good technique" with nothing observable in it |
| Fairness | The learner knows what is expected and has a reasonable chance to show it | Instructions that contradict each other. A word guide the learner cannot meet in the space provided. No reasonable adjustment pathway |
| Flexibility | Fits the learner and the delivery context | A single assessment occasion for work that cannot be done in one. No recognition pathway where one is plausible |

## Rules of evidence

| Rule | What it requires | What a breach looks like in a tool |
|---|---|---|
| Valid | The evidence relates to the requirement claimed against it | An observation sheet mapped only to elements and performance criteria, so nothing records which task carried which technique |
| Sufficient | Enough evidence, across enough occasions, to be confident | One observation of a skill the unit requires repeatedly. A referenced observation form that was never written |
| Authentic | The evidence is the learner's own work | A coverage matrix the learner completes themselves, so the claim is learner-asserted rather than assessor-verified |
| Current | Reflects current skills and current industry practice | A tool citing a superseded unit release, repealed legislation, or a workplace practice no longer used |

The line that makes both lists stick: the principles are about the assessment, the rules are about the evidence it collects. You can have a valid assessment that collects insufficient evidence, and a tool that gathers mountains of evidence against the wrong requirement.

## What changed from the 2015 Standards

**Outcomes-based.** The old Standards specified process. The 2025 Standards specify what has to be true and leave the method to the RTO. More freedom and more exposure — "we followed the process" is not a defence if the method does not produce the outcome.

**The validation quota is gone.** The 2015 requirement to validate 50% of training products within three years is replaced by a five-year cycle per product, brought forward by risk, by a change to the product, or by feedback.

**"Designed" is new.** A validation outcome cannot be determined solely by a person who *designed* the assessment, as well as one who delivered or assessed it. The 2015 wording covered delivery and assessment only. In a small RTO where one person writes most of the tools, this materially changes who can sit on a panel — worth saying out loud, because it will be news to someone in the room.

## Questions that always come up

**"If I wrote the tool, can I do the pre-use review of it?"**
Yes for 1.3(b) — nothing bars the author from reviewing their own tool before use, and in a small team there is often nobody else. No for determining a 1.5 validation outcome on your own. The practical answer is to get a second reader for the pre-use review anyway, because you cannot see your own assumptions.

**"Isn't this what validation is?"**
No, and conflating them is why RTOs arrive at audit having done one and believing they did both.

**"We use a purchased pack, so isn't this the vendor's problem?"**
A purchased or inherited tool becomes the RTO's responsibility the moment it is issued.

## Claims to avoid

Do not state a specific clause letter or sub-numbering beyond what is verified here without checking it. Getting a clause reference wrong in front of an audience of assessors costs the session its authority.

Do not present sample sizes, retention periods or frequency rules as regulatory requirements when they are RTO policy. The two-unit minimum for validating a qualification is ASQA's; most sample-size bands are the RTO's own and should be labelled as such.

Do not teach governance, credential policy, fit-and-proper-person or self-assurance content into a trainer and assessor session. It is all real and none of it is this audience's job. Park it and offer a follow-up session.

## Alternative session topics

If assessment tool review is not the gap the user wants to close:

**Trainer currency and CPD evidence.** Trainer files are consistently among the most common audit findings. Works well as a session because participants can audit their own file in the activity slot.

**Feedback, complaints and learner support** under the wellbeing standards. Suits a mixed audience of trainers and student support staff better than the assessment topic does.

**Validation of judgements (Standard 1.5).** The natural sequel once a group has the 1.3(b) session behind them. Needs completed learner work as the activity material, so it can only run for a unit with a finished cohort.
