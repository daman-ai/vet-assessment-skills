**1. Industry Induction & WHS**

`CPCCOM1013`, `CPCCOM1015`

### The rulings in full

#### CPC20220-T01 &mdash; Reporting hazards, incidents and injuries

*progressive-depth* &middot; taught in full by **CPCWHS1001**

**Why here.** CPCWHS1001 is the White Card - the statutory general construction induction - and is delivered in the first week of Term 1. Hazard and incident reporting is content the induction is required by law to cover, so a learner meets it there before anything else. CPCCWHS2001 follows in the same cluster and goes deeper.

**What the other units do.** CPCCWHS2001 does not re-teach what a hazard is or why it is reported. It teaches the DELTA: the organisation's own reporting procedure, the forms and registers that record it, and the notifiable-incident duty that sends a report to the regulator rather than to a supervisor.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCWHS1001 KE: identifying and reporting hazards, incidents and injuries in the workplace

#### CPC20220-T02 &mdash; Personal protective equipment: selection, fitting, use and care

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCCM2012**

**Why here.** PPE is named by three units. CPCCCM2012 Work safely at heights, delivered first in Term 3, carries the fullest statement of it - selection, fitting AND use - because fall-arrest equipment is the case where fitting actually decides whether the equipment works. Teaching it there gives the plastering units a fitted-PPE standard to apply rather than a second, thinner account of the same thing.

**What the other units do.** CPCCSP2002 and CPCCSP2003 name the PPE their task requires and the reason for it - eye protection against splashback, respiratory protection against silica dust - and do not restate selection or fitting.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCCM2012 KE: selection, fitting and use of personal protective equipment (PPE)

#### CPC20220-T03 &mdash; Safety data sheets and hazardous material information

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCCM2012**

**Why here.** Reading an SDS is one skill regardless of the substance. It is owned in the same unit as PPE because the two are read together: the SDS is what tells a worker which PPE the substance requires.

**What the other units do.** The plastering units point at the SDS for the specific product in front of the learner - cement, lime, additives - and read the exposure and first-aid sections from it. They do not re-teach the sixteen-section structure of the document.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCCM2012 KE: safety data sheets (SDSs)

#### CPC20220-T04 &mdash; Job safety analyses and safe work method statements

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP2002**

**Why here.** Three units require a JSA or SWMS prepared to the Safe Work Australia Code of Practice for Construction Work. CPCCSP2002, first in Term 3, is the earliest of them, and a SWMS is a document a learner writes once and then reuses with a different task in the task column.

**What the other units do.** CPCCSP2003, CPCCCO2013 and CPCCVE1011 each produce a SWMS for their own task. They are assessed on the CONTENT being right for that task, not on knowing what a SWMS is. The risk-control reasoning behind it belongs to CPCCWHS2001 in Cluster 1 and is cited, not repeated.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP2002 KE: safety requirements for using solid plastering tools and equipment, including:job safety analyses and/or safe work method statements in accordance with Safe Work Australia Code of Practice for Construction Work or state and/or territory equivalent

#### CPC20220-T05 &mdash; Workplace quality policies and standards for trade work

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP2002**

**Why here.** What 'to standard' means - tolerance, finish, rectification, sign-off - is a single idea shared by the two plastering units, and CPCCSP2002 is delivered first.

**What the other units do.** CPCCSP2003 states the tolerance that applies to a prepared surface and moves on.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP2002 KE: workplace quality policies and standards for using solid plastering tools and equipment

#### CPC20220-T06 &mdash; National Construction Code and Australian Standards compliance

*regulatory-recall* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCCO2013**

**Why here.** Both Term 4 units cite the NCC and Australian Standards. The TAS delivery sequence puts CPCCCO2013 before CPCCVE1011, so the concreting unit introduces what the Code is, how a Standard is called up by it, and how to find the clause that governs a task. The capstone project unit then applies it. NOTE: the detector suggested CPCCVE1011 because it sits earlier in the TAS unit TABLE; the delivery sequence is the authority and it says otherwise.

**What the other units do.** CPCCVE1011 identifies the clauses relevant to its own project and demonstrates compliance. It does not re-explain the Code's structure.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCCO2013 KE: compliance requirements of the National Construction Code and Australian Standards relevant to concreting to simple forms
