## Themes

**1. Site safety and workplace communication** (weeks 1-12)

Enter and work safely on a construction site, plan daily work and communicate with the site team.

`CPCWHS1001`, `CPCCOM1012`, `CPCCWHS2001`, `CPCCOM1013`, `CPCCOM1014`

**2. Plans, levelling and safe access** (weeks 13-24)

Read plans and specifications, set out levels and measurements, and erect restricted height scaffold to work safely above ground.

`CPCCOM2001`, `CPCCOM1015`, `CPCCCM2006`, `CPCCCM2008`, `CPCCCM2012`

**3. Plastering tools, materials and surface preparation** (weeks 25-32)

Select, mix and handle plastering materials and prepare masonry and framed surfaces ready for coating.

`CPCCSP2002`, `CPCCSP2001`, `CPCCSP2003`

**4. Render, set and texture finishes** (weeks 33-44)

Float and render straight and curved surfaces, apply set and trowelled texture coats, and restore existing solid plasterwork.

`CPCCSP3001`, `CPCCSP3002`, `CPCCSP3003`, `CPCCSP3004`

**5. Concreting and business start-up** (weeks 45-52)

Place concrete to simple forms and cost, finance and plan a small plastering business.

`CPCCCO2013`, `BSBESB301`, `BSBESB407`

### The rulings in full

#### CPC31020-T01 &mdash; Reporting hazards, incidents and injuries

*progressive-depth* &middot; taught in full by **CPCWHS1001**

**Why here.** Cluster 1 is delivered in Term 1 and opens with the White Card. The statutory induction covers identifying and reporting hazards before any other unit runs.

**What the other units do.** CPCCWHS2001 teaches the delta - the organisation's procedure, the register, and the notifiable-incident duty. It does not restate what a hazard is.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCWHS1001 KE: identifying and reporting hazards, incidents and injuries in the workplace

#### CPC31020-T02 &mdash; Personal protective equipment: selection, fitting, use and care

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCCM2012**

**Why here.** Eight units name PPE. CPCCCM2012 Work safely at heights in Cluster 3 carries the fullest statement - selection, fitting and use - and fall-arrest gear is the case that makes fitting matter. Every plastering unit downstream then has a fitted-PPE standard to apply.

**What the other units do.** Each plastering unit names the PPE its own task demands and the hazard it controls - silica dust on mixing, splashback on rendering, dermatitis on lime handling - in a line or two. Selection and fitting are not repeated.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCCM2012 KE: selection, fitting and use of personal protective equipment (PPE)

#### CPC31020-T03 &mdash; Safety data sheets and hazardous material information

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCCM2008**

**Why here.** Eight units require an SDS to be read. CPCCCM2008 Erect and dismantle restricted height scaffolding is the earliest of them in the delivery order, sitting in Cluster 3 ahead of the whole plastering stream.

**What the other units do.** Plastering units open the SDS for the actual product - cement, hydrated lime, additives - and read the exposure, PPE and first-aid sections. The document's structure is not re-taught.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCCM2008 KE: safety date sheets (SDSs)

#### CPC31020-T04 &mdash; Job safety analyses and safe work method statements for plastering work

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP2001**

**Why here.** Seven plastering units require a JSA or SWMS to the Safe Work Australia Code of Practice for Construction Work. CPCCSP2001 Handle solid plastering materials opens Cluster 4 and is the first of them.

**What the other units do.** Each later unit writes a SWMS for its own task and is assessed on whether the controls fit that task. The risk-assessment reasoning behind it is owned by CPCCWHS2001 in Cluster 1 and is cited. CPCCSP2002 is outside this topic and writes its own plant SWMS - test and tag, guarding, pre-start checks, start-up and shut-down - because none of that appears in the materials-handling case.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP2001 KE: safety requirements for handling solid plastering materials, including:job safety analyses and/or safe work method statements in accordance with Safe Work Australia Code of Practice for Construction Work or state and/or territory equivalent

#### CPC31020-T05 &mdash; Plastering materials: compounds, finishing coats and additives

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP2001**

**Why here.** Two families - additives such as plasticisers, colouring and waterproofing agents, and plaster compounds and finishing coats - are one body of material science, and CPCCSP2001 Handle solid plastering materials is the unit whose entire subject it is. Five units restate parts of it.

**What the other units do.** CPCCSP2003, CPCCSP3001, CPCCSP3002 and CPCCSP3004 select the mix appropriate to their own application and justify the choice. They do not re-teach what an additive does.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP2001 KE: plaster compounds and finishing coats
- CPCCSP2001 KE: additives, such as plasticisers, colour and waterproofing agents

#### CPC31020-T06 &mdash; Backing materials: timber and expanded metal lathing

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP2001**

**Why here.** Lathing is a material, and materials are owned by the materials unit in Cluster 4, ahead of the application units that fix plaster to it.

**What the other units do.** CPCCSP3001 and CPCCSP3002 teach fixing and keying to lath, not what lath is.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP2001 KE: timber or expanded metal lathing (EML)

#### CPC31020-T07 &mdash; Components and accessories: corner beads, stop beads and trims

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP3001**

**Why here.** Beads and trims are set out as part of applying float and render, which is CPCCSP3001, the first unit of Cluster 5. CPCCSP3002 and CPCCSP3004 work to beads that are already in place.

**What the other units do.** Later units check bead alignment as a set-out condition and do not re-teach the component range.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP3001 KE: uses of materials and components for solid plastering, including:corner beads

#### CPC31020-T08 &mdash; Traditional lime materials: sand, lime and lime putty

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP3001**

**Why here.** Lime is distinct from the cement-based compounds owned by CPCCSP2001 - it behaves differently, sets differently and is what heritage work uses. It is introduced where it is first applied, in CPCCSP3001, and is what makes CPCCSP3004 Restore and renovate solid plasterwork possible.

**What the other units do.** CPCCSP3004 teaches matching an existing historic mix and is assessed on the match, not on what lime putty is.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP3001 KE: sand, lime and lime putty

#### CPC31020-T09 &mdash; Workplace quality policies and standards for plastering work

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP2001**

**Why here.** What finished to standard means - tolerance, flatness, plumb, rectification and sign-off - is one idea, owned at the start of the plastering stream.

**What the other units do.** Each application unit states the tolerance that governs its own finish and moves on. CPCCSP2002 is not in this topic: its quality standard is about tool condition, tagging and storage, not about whether a finished surface passes.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP2001 KE: workplace quality policies and standards for handling solid plastering materials

#### CPC31020-T10 &mdash; Solid plastering tools and equipment: identification, use, care and maintenance

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP2002**

**Why here.** CPCCSP2002 Use solid plastering tools and equipment is the unit whose entire subject is the tools, and it sits alongside CPCCSP2001 in Cluster 4. The detector suggested CPCCSP2001 because it is listed first, but a tool is taught in the tools unit; the materials unit only needs to name the barrows, buckets and mixers it handles materials with.

**What the other units do.** CPCCSP2001 names the materials-handling equipment it uses and cites CPCCSP2002 for care, maintenance and safe use.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP2002 KE: environmental requirements for using solid plastering tools and equipment
- CPCCSP2002 KE: manufacturers’ specifications for solid plastering tools and equipment

#### CPC31020-T11 &mdash; Proprietary texture coat finish products

*shared-scaffold* &middot; taught in full by **CPCCSP3003**

**Why here.** CPCCSP3003 Apply trowelled texture coat finishes owns proprietary texture systems - the product range, the manufacturer specification and the application method it dictates.

**What the other units do.** CPCCSP3004 matches an existing texture during restoration and cites CPCCSP3003 for the product knowledge.

Anchored to training.gov.au:

- CPCCSP3003 KE: proprietary texture coat finishes products
