# Simulated work experience — Section B of the combined UAT

Where the unit does **not** require food to be produced, the pack is **two documents**: one `[UNIT]_UAT.docx` carrying Section A Knowledge and Section B Performance, and one assessor guide mirroring it.

The practical evidence stays in **Section B of that UAT**, with each observation checklist and observation record following the task it observes. There is no separate practical workbook and no assessor evidence pack — those exist on the food branch because a recipe workbook needs its own book.

This file is what stops Section B becoming three disconnected exercises that happen to share a venue name. The learner should experience it as **one shift, one job or one project** — the work a person in that role actually does, in the order they do it.

Read with `compliance-rules.md` section 5, which carries the task and checklist rules. This file adds the coherence layer on top.

---

## 1. When this applies

Apply the recipe-workbook trigger test in `recipe-workbook.md` section 1 first. If the Performance Evidence does not require producing food items to standard recipes, this is the branch - and the pack is two documents, not four.

That includes plenty of practical, hands-on units — a construction unit surveying a site, a business unit running a consultation, a hospitality unit doing a bar service or a stock take. It also includes units that are almost entirely knowledge-based, where Section B may be a single small task or, occasionally, nothing at all.

**Where the unit's Performance Evidence requires no demonstrated performance, say so plainly** in the assessment overview and carry no Section B tasks. Do not invent a practical task to balance the document. The pack is still two documents - the UAT simply has a short Section B.

---

## 2. One operational context, not three tasks

Section B draws on the **same scenario world** as Section A — the contract's scenario card. This section adds a spine to it.

**Choose the spine from the work itself:**

| Unit shape | Spine |
|---|---|
| A trade or production unit | one job on one site, from setup to handover |
| A service unit | one shift, from pre-service through service to close |
| A planning or supervisory unit | one project or one reporting cycle, from brief to sign-off |
| A compliance or admin unit | one case or one audit, from trigger to outcome |

Every task is a stage of that one spine. Task 2 picks up where Task 1 left off, and uses what Task 1 produced. Where Task 1 has the learner produce a plan, Task 2 has them work to that plan — not to a fresh plan the document hands them.

**This is a validity point, not a presentation preference.** A unit whose Performance Criteria run *plan → consult → implement → review* is not evidenced by three tasks that each start from a clean sheet. The sequencing is part of what the unit assesses.

### The seam to watch

Where a later task depends on a learner's own earlier output, the assessor needs a fallback for a learner whose Task 1 output is Not Satisfactory. State it in the assessor guide: the assessor issues a **controlled baseline version** of the deliverable so the learner can be assessed on Task 2 on its own merits, and the Task 1 result stands separately.

Without this, one weak deliverable cascades into three Not Satisfactory results for what is really a single gap.

---

## 3. Sequencing mirrors the real work order

Order the tasks the way the job is actually done. Where the unit's Elements are already in work order, follow them. Where they are not — and Elements are often grouped by theme rather than sequence — follow the **work**, and note in the mapping that Task 2 covers Element 3 because that is when the activity happens on the day.

Do not reorder the *unit*. Reorder the *tasks*, and map honestly.

**Name the timeframe on every task's `Maps to` row**, and make the timeframes add up to something a real shift could hold. Three tasks each nominally taking a full day, assessed in one four-hour observation, is a condition the assessment cannot meet.

---

## 4. The environment requirement governs everything

Capture the unit's environment statement **verbatim** at extraction — for example *"Skills must be demonstrated in an operational business environment. This can be: an industry workplace; or an industry-realistic simulated environment."*

That sentence governs Section B, the assessment conditions block and every alternative pathway. It is **not negotiable**:

- **Do not offer a documentary case study, desk exercise or written project as a standalone alternative** where the unit requires demonstration in an operational environment. Supplied documentation may supplement evidence; it cannot replace the demonstration.
- A recorded **description** of what the learner would do is not acceptable. A recording made **while** the learner performs the tasks in the required environment is.
- Where the unit demands interaction with customers, staff or other professionals, build that interaction into the task rather than describing it.

Where the environment statement permits a simulated environment, say in the assessment conditions **how the simulation meets it** — what makes it industry-realistic, in the unit's own terms.

---

## 5. What the simulation needs, and where it lives

Everything a learner may see goes in the **detailed scenario** in the UAT front matter. Everything else goes in the **assessor guide setup pack**. Nothing crosses that line.

### In the learner document

- The business, its type, size and location
- The learner's role and who they work with, each named person carrying their role
- The documents available to them — every one a task refers to, present or precisely identified
- The constraints: volume, time, access, budget, conditions
- The situation on the day
- A bold `Note:` line for anything the learner must or must not assume

Written in enough detail that a learner can complete Section B without asking a question, and held to one page.

### In the assessor guide setup pack

- The documents to plant in the workplace folder, with the content each must carry
- The physical cues to stage — **one mandatory core cue per required category, identical for every learner.** Further cues are optional and never substituted for a core one
- Role-player briefs: what each person says, what they withhold, and what they do if pressed
- Event timing: what happens when, and what triggers it
- Confirmation that other personnel are briefed and the mandated external resources are on hand

### Three rules for the setup pack

They apply here in full and are stated once, in `compliance-rules.md` section 7: **the assessment must not create the hazard it assesses**; **staging controls are not the learner's controls**; **check the pack against itself**.

---

## 6. Role players

Where the unit requires consultation, negotiation, instruction, handover or customer service, a role player carries evidence. Brief them properly or the observation is unreliable.

Each brief states:

- **Who they are** — name and role, matching the scenario card exactly
- **What they want**, and what they will accept
- **What they know that the learner does not**, and what makes them reveal it
- **How they respond to a competent approach, and to a poor one** — a role player who cooperates regardless produces no evidence of the learner's consultation skill
- **What they must not do** — lead the learner, supply the answer, or escalate beyond the brief

**Keep role-player behaviour identical for every learner.** Two learners who met differently-behaved role players were not assessed against the same standard, and that is a reliability defect.

---

## 7. Contingent criteria

The rule is `compliance-rules.md` section 5: where a checklist carries a criterion that responds to an event, the assessor guide **names the trigger**, the assessor sets it up before the session and records which was used, and **a criterion with no trigger and no natural opportunity is removed rather than left to chance**.

On this branch the events are typically reporting a fault, identifying a non-conformance, escalating a decision outside the learner's scope, or responding to a change in requirements.

---

## 8. Contextualise to South Australia

The instruments, the settings and the named suburbs are all in `sa-legislation.md` — use only the instruments the unit actually calls up, and place the scenario somewhere real and appropriate to the vocation, with realistic constraints of volume, time, access, budget and stakeholders.

**Where the tool names a simulated competitor, supplier or client, label it as simulated.** One file calling a competitor fictional while another tells learners to research them as a real business is a known defect pattern, and the compliance review looks for it.

---

## 9. Where a workplace pathway is offered

The pathway rules — the assessor pre-check, the pathway-neutral checklist, and pathway-consistent wording throughout — are in `compliance-rules.md` section 6. **No third-party report is built** — section 10 of that file. Two things this branch adds:

- The **industry substitution table** goes in the assessment conditions, and maps each simulation artefact and role to its workplace equivalent: the organisational plan, each named procedure, the templates, forms and registers, the other personnel, the approver, the site areas.
- Do not require simulation-specific staffing the unit does not mandate.

---

## 10. Report on the build

`compliance-rules.md` section 11 carries what every build reports. On this branch, add:

- The trigger decision — why this unit took the non-food branch rather than the food branch, and therefore why the pack is two documents
- The spine chosen and why it fits the work
- Which task depends on which earlier output, and the controlled baseline the assessor issues where an earlier task was Not Satisfactory
- Every core cue, its category, and the genuine learner action left available against it
- Every role player, and confirmation the brief fixes their behaviour across learners
- Every contingent criterion and its named trigger
- Confirmation the setup pack does not contradict itself — no cue depends on a document also placed in the learner folder
- The environment statement verbatim, and how the simulation meets it
