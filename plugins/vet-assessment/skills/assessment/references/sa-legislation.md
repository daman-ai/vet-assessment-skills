# South Australian legislative context

**Use only the instruments the attached unit actually calls up.** Do not bolt on irrelevant Acts to look thorough — an auditor reads padding as padding, and a wrong-jurisdiction or superseded citation is a finding in its own right.

Where the unit names an instrument, cite it. Where the unit describes an obligation without naming the instrument, name the SA instrument that carries it. Where the unit calls up nothing legislative, cite nothing.

## Hospitality, cookery and food handling (SIT)

| Instrument | Regulator |
|---|---|
| Food Act 2001 (SA) | Local council environmental health officers; SA Health |
| Food Regulations 2017 (SA) | As above |
| Food Standards Code (FSANZ) Standards 3.2.2, 3.2.2A, 3.2.3 | FSANZ; enforced by councils and SA Health |
| Food Standards Code Standard 1.2.3 — allergen declaration (PEAL) | As above |
| Liquor Licensing Act 1997 (SA) and Responsible Service of Alcohol | Consumer and Business Services |

Working figures where the unit supports them: temperature danger zone 5–60 °C; refrigerated holding 1–4 °C; HACCP-based food safety programs.

Note that some states require a business to designate a certified food safety supervisor. Where the unit says so, reflect it — do not assert it where the unit does not.

**Superseded — do not cite.** The Development Act 1993 was replaced by the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016. Citing it is a compliance finding.

## Training packages not listed

Identify the SA-specific Acts, regulators and standards that genuinely apply to that vocation and use those. Do not fall back on the general list alone and do not guess. If you cannot establish the instrument with confidence, say so and ask.

## Scenarios

Use realistic Adelaide-based settings appropriate to the unit's vocation:

- **Hospitality** — a CBD restaurant kitchen, a function venue, a suburban café
- **Business** — a simulated small business or student-services setting
- **Trades** — a named suburb: Magill, Norwood, Mile End, Mount Barker, Glenelg, Prospect, Adelaide CBD

Give the scenario realistic constraints of volume, time, access, budget and stakeholders.

**Use one scenario world across the whole tool** — the same venue, the same personnel, the same documents in Part A and Part B. A learner who has to hold two fictional workplaces in mind is being tested on memory, not competence.

## Currency

Legislation changes. Before citing an instrument, confirm it is the current one and that the citation — name, year, jurisdiction — is exact. Where the unit itself references something that has since been superseded, cite the current instrument and record the correction in the assessor guide.
